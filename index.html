How

  1. Read. Each model reads your whole repository, README, pitch and video transcript on its own.
  2. Check. It lists every claim in your README and traces it to the code.
  3. Score. Eight criteria, 1 to 5, each score with a quote from your files. No quote, no score.
  4. Compare. Every project against every other, in pairs, both orders, all four models, one question: which is the better hackathon project.
  5. Rank. Your share of won comparisons is your score from the models. Judges never see each other's names; disagreement stays visible on your scorecard.

Nothing is executed. What the README, the code and the video show is what counts.

Knockouts: no code behind any claim and no demo; or work from before the event that was not declared. Three judges must confirm a knockout with quotes.

The overall question for every comparison: which of the two is the better hackathon project? One verified astonishing thing, real fun, or real value to NetBird users can each carry a project. Relevance to NetBird lifts a good project, it never replaces quality.

What you submit

  • Your GitHub repository link. We judge it as it is at the deadline.
  • A short demo video.
  • Two or three sentences: what it is, for whom, what works today.
  • Private repository: add github.com/philipvollet as a reader.

Declare anything that existed before the hackathon in your README. Commit as you go; a single giant upload at the end and large binaries count against you.

What you get back

Your scorecard with every judge's scores and quotes, the ranking of the field, and the judge prompts after the event.
